A wagering requirement is the amount you must bet before a casino releases bonus money, or the winnings from it, for withdrawal. It is written as a multiplier, such as 10x or 15x, of a set amount: the bonus, your deposit, or both. A 10x requirement on a $100 bonus means $1,000 in bets. If the game you play counts only partly, for example blackjack at 10%, you have to bet ten times more. Use our [wagering calculator](/tools/wagering-calculator/) to work out your own total.

## How to calculate a wagering requirement

**Total to bet = amount the requirement applies to × multiplier ÷ game contribution**

| Example | Calculation | Total to bet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $100 bonus, 10x the bonus, slots (100%) | $100 × 10 ÷ 1 | $1,000 |
| $100 deposit, 15x the deposit, slots (100%) | $100 × 15 ÷ 1 | $1,500 |
| $100 bonus, 10x the bonus, blackjack (10%) | $100 × 10 ÷ 0.1 | $10,000 |

The first two rows use the multipliers from real Ontario offers below. The third shows why the game you choose matters as much as the multiplier.

## Game contribution: why your game choice matters

Each game type counts towards the requirement at a set rate. A 10% rate means a $10 bet counts as $1.

## The rules that matter more than the multiplier

- **The time limit.** If you do not finish in time, the bonus and its winnings are removed. OLG gives 168 hours; LeoVegas Ontario gives 30 days for its casino welcome offer and 7 days for its live casino one.
- **Withdrawing early.** At OLG, withdrawing any part of your cash balance automatically removes all unconverted bonuses and the winnings from them. LeoVegas Ontario says you can withdraw your real-money balance without losing its casino welcome offer. Check this rule before you request a payout.
- **Which money is used first.** At OLG, your cash balance is used first and bonus funds only when the cash runs out.
- **Caps.** Offers have a maximum: up to $1,500 for LeoVegas Ontario's casino welcome offer and up to $1,000 for OLG's 2025 match-deposit bonus.

## What Ontario rules say about bonuses

Ontario casinos licensed by the AGCO must follow its standards on advertising bonuses:

- **Where you can see offers.** Bonus advertising is allowed only on the casino's own site and in direct marketing you have agreed to receive. Public advertising of bonuses, including targeted online ads, is not allowed.
- **Conditions up front.** An offer must show all its material conditions and limitations when it is first presented on the site, with the rest no more than one click away.
- **No fake "free".** An offer cannot be called free if you have to risk or lose your own money or if conditions apply to your own money. It cannot be called risk-free if you must risk your own money to use or withdraw winnings.
- **Reasonably attainable.** Casinos may not offer a promotion that cannot reasonably be achieved without substantial losses.

If an Ontario offer hides its wagering terms or calls a conditional bonus "free", it breaks these standards, and you can raise it with the AGCO.

## Should you take a bonus at all?

You do not have to. A bonus with a wagering requirement can stop you withdrawing your own money until the requirement is met or the bonus is removed, depending on the casino's rules. If your priority is a quick payout, playing without a bonus is the simplest option. If you do take one, read the multiplier, what it applies to, the time limit and the early-withdrawal rule before you deposit.
