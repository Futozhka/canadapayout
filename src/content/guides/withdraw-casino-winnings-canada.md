The request first sits as "pending" while the casino reviews it, then the payment provider or your bank moves the money. ## How long does a casino withdrawal take?

A withdrawal has two stages, and most "slow payout" complaints are about the first one.

- **Pending (casino review).** The casino checks your identity, your bonus terms and the request itself. [CHECK: typical pending time at 2–3 Ontario operators]
- **Transfer (payment provider and bank).** Once approved, the money moves. For a bank transfer from OLG this typically takes 3–5 business days.

Bank transfers are counted in business days, so weekends and holidays add time.

## Why is my withdrawal delayed or declined?

- **Your account or identity is not verified.** Casinos verify who you are to meet FINTRAC rules. At OLG you cannot withdraw until your bank account is verified.
- **Your documents do not match.** [CHECK: confirm in 2–3 operators' KYC pages that names and addresses must match.] An old bank document will not be accepted: OLG asks for one dated within the last two months.

## What to do if your withdrawal is stuck

1. **Check the status in your account.** If it is still pending, the casino has not approved it yet. At OLG, for example, a pending request can be cancelled from Pending Withdrawals.
2. **Contact support and ask exactly what is missing.** Ask whether any document is outstanding and when the review will be finished. Save the chat or email.
3. **File a formal complaint with the casino.** Use its official complaints process and keep the complaint number.
4. **Wait for the casino's decision.** In Ontario and Alberta this can take up to 90 days.
5. **Go to the regulator if the casino does not resolve it.**
   - Ontario: file a complaint with the AGCO through its iAGCO online service. You will need the site name, your player ID and the casino's complaint number.
   - Alberta: contact AGLC. Its first review takes about 3 business days; a full compliance review can take up to 5 weeks, and complex cases 3–6 months.

Regulators cannot get your money back for you. In Ontario, the AGCO can penalize a casino that broke the rules, from warnings to suspensions and fines. That is a strong reason to play only at casinos licensed in your province.

## Are casino winnings taxed in Canada?

For recreational players, generally no. Gambling winnings are not taxable in Canada and do not go on your tax return, unless you gamble as a business. Interest or investment income you later earn on the winnings is taxable. [CHECK: quote the Canada.ca page "Amounts that are not reported or taxed"]

Winning in the United States is different. US casinos usually withhold 30% of gambling winnings from Canadian residents. You can get some or all of it back by filing a US non-resident tax return (Form 1040NR), with Form W-7 to get a tax ID number if you do not have one. Losses can be used to reduce the taxable amount.
