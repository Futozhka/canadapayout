To withdraw casino winnings in Canada, verify your identity and the bank account you will be paid into, open the cashier, choose a withdrawal method such as Interac e-Transfer or a bank transfer, and submit the amount. The request then sits as "pending" while the casino reviews it, and after approval the payment provider or your bank moves the money. In Ontario there is no fixed deadline: casinos must pay out "as soon as is practicable". A bank transfer from OLG, for example, typically takes 3–5 business days after approval.

## How to withdraw, step by step

1. **Verify your account before you need the money.** Upload your ID and proof of the bank account you will be paid into. At OLG this is a bank statement, void cheque or cleared cheque dated within the last two months. Doing this early is the easiest way to avoid delays.
2. **Read the withdrawal rules once.** Ontario casinos must tell you about every rule and restriction on withdrawals and every fee before you withdraw. Look for the minimum amount, any fee, and which methods you can withdraw to.
3. **Open the cashier and choose Withdraw.** Pick a method that is listed for withdrawals, not only for deposits.
4. **Enter the amount and submit.** You will see the request as pending.
5. **Wait for approval.** The casino reviews the request and may ask for more documents.
6. **Receive the money.** After approval, the time depends on the method and on your bank.

## How long does a casino withdrawal take?

A withdrawal has two stages, and most "slow payout" complaints are about the first one.

- **Pending (casino review).** The casino checks that you are the account holder, that the money goes to an account you hold, and whether any bonus terms still apply. The AGCO standards set no number of days here; they require the casino to process withdrawals "as soon as is practicable".
- **Transfer (payment provider and bank).** Once approved, the money moves. For a bank transfer from OLG this typically takes 3–5 business days.

Bank transfers are counted in business days, so weekends and holidays add time.

## Why is my withdrawal delayed or declined?

- **Your identity or bank account is not verified yet.** Ontario casinos may pay out only "after the appropriate verifications", and identity checks follow Canada's anti-money-laundering law. At OLG you cannot withdraw until your bank account is verified.
- **The account is not in your name.** The money must go to an account of which you are a legal holder. A partner's or friend's account will not work. An out-of-date document can also be refused: OLG asks for a bank document dated within the last two months.
- **A bonus is still active.** If you accepted a bonus, its conditions may limit when you can withdraw. Ontario casinos must show all material conditions of an offer when they first present it, so check what you agreed to. Our [wagering calculator](/tools/wagering-calculator/) shows how much is left to bet, and [wagering requirements explained](/guides/wagering-requirements-explained/) covers the rules that can block a withdrawal.
- **The method you chose is not allowed for this withdrawal.** Some rules tie withdrawals to particular methods. The casino must spell these rules out, so read the Withdrawals section of its terms.
- **Your bank blocked or limited the payment.** Interac limits are set by your bank.

## What to do if your withdrawal is stuck

1. **Check the status in your account.** If it is still pending, the casino has not approved it yet. At OLG, for example, a pending request can be cancelled from Pending Withdrawals.
2. **Contact support and ask exactly what is missing.** Ask whether any document is outstanding and when the review will be finished. Save the chat or email.
3. **File a formal complaint with the casino.** Use its official complaints process and keep the complaint number.
4. **Wait for the casino's decision.** In Ontario and Alberta this can take up to 90 days.
5. **Go to the regulator if the casino does not resolve it.**
   - Ontario: file a complaint with the AGCO through its iAGCO online service. You will need the site name, your player ID and the casino's complaint number.
   - Alberta: contact AGLC. Its first review takes about 3 business days; a full compliance review can take up to 5 weeks, and complex cases 3–6 months.

Regulators cannot get your money back for you. In Ontario, the AGCO can penalize a casino that broke the rules, from warnings to suspensions and fines. That is a strong reason to play only at casinos licensed in your province.

## Are casino winnings taxed in Canada?

For recreational players, generally no. The CRA lists "lottery winnings of any amount" among the amounts you do not report on your tax return, unless the prize is income from employment, a business or property. Gambling as a business is the main exception. Income you later earn on the winnings is taxable: the CRA's own example is interest on invested lottery winnings.

Winning in the United States is different. US casinos usually withhold 30% of gambling winnings from Canadian residents. You can get some or all of it back by filing a US non-resident tax return (Form 1040NR), with Form W-7 to get a tax ID number if you do not have one. Losses can be used to reduce the taxable amount.
